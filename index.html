Abstract

Children's earliest years are a period of intense social learning, during which meaning is built through gaze, gesture, and physical contact. Yet this critical aspect of social interaction remains under-evaluated in multimodal video understanding. We introduce NEST (Naturalistic Early-childhood Social inTeractions), the first child-centric benchmark for nonverbal social reasoning — 1,208 manually annotated clips (~12s) with 14,025 structured questions, organized by a compositional schema spanning contextual, behavioral, interpersonal, and open-ended reasoning.
